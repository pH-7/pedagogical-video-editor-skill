# Editorial guide

## Long-form learning rhythm

Think in learning beats rather than arbitrary seconds: promise, concept, explanation, example, contrast, application, recap. Let complex ideas breathe. Tighten repeated starts, searches for words, setup friction, and pauses that carry no thought. Preserve intentional silence after a difficult idea.

Use visual change when it helps the viewer encode or retrieve information. Talking head can remain stable during a nuanced explanation; a definition may benefit from a clean term card; a process benefits from a progressive diagram; a comparison benefits from a two-column visual; a concrete example benefits from screen detail or B-roll.

## Cuts and coverage

- Keep short handles around speech when removing silence.
- Avoid cutting during blinks, consonants, large gestures, or room-noise changes.
- Cover jump cuts with relevant visuals or a restrained crop change.
- If a cut changes ambience, use a short audio crossfade or room tone.
- Never reorder claims unless the revised logic is verified against the full transcript.

## Zooms

Use `zoom` events for emphasis or reframing. Typical talking-head values are 1.04–1.10 over 0.4–1.2 seconds, held briefly, then returned in a later event. Stronger values require sufficient source resolution and a clear reason. Check eyes, hands, captions, and diagrams remain in frame.

## Captions and French

Keep captions faithful, not paraphrased. Aim for one or two lines, natural phrase boundaries, and readable exposure time. French typography should preserve accents and use appropriate spacing before `: ; ? !` where the chosen caption renderer and font support it. Keep terminology consistent.

## Sound

Target intelligible, stable dialogue first. Add music only when it supports energy or structure. Use sparse, low-level cues for a reveal, transition, or visual arrival. Listen on headphones and laptop speakers. Automated loudness data is evidence, not a replacement for listening.

## Reference analysis

Analyse several authorised examples when possible. Record ranges rather than copying one video: average scene-change interval, speech/pause ratio, use of close-ups, visual-aid density, caption density, music presence, and section rhythm. Convert observations into principles appropriate to the current audience and subject.
